    Spider-Man: No Way Home trivia

    Background details, easter eggs and behind-the-scenes facts fans have spotted in Spider-Man: No Way Home.

    Scene detailsMCU connectionsComics referencesPop culture

    Scene details

    • Eddie Brock / Venom from Sony's Spider-Man Universe appears in the film's post-credits scene where he's talking to a bartender about important individuals and events in the MCU Earth. After he's sent back to his home dimension, a speck of the Venom symbiote is left behind. In the scene, upon hearing the name "Hulk", Venom remarks to Eddie "And you thought 'Lethal Protector' was a shit name!", referencing the events of Venom: Let There Be Carnage, in which Venom suggests he and Eddie become a vigilante by that name. "Venom: Lethal Protector" is also the name of a 1993 comic book series about Venom.
    • The events of Spider-Man: No Way Home are mentioned in the Sony Pictures Animation film Spider-Man: Across the Spider-Verse. This marks the first time an animated project outside of the Marvel Studios territory mentions the events of an MCU film.
    • There are several lines from the trailers that were re-worked or entirely cut in the finished film. Dr. Strange's first discussion with Peter about using his first name is different. Additionally, in the trailer Wong tells Dr. Strange not cast the spell, and Strange promises he won't. This is entirely cut from the final film.
    • On December 8, 2021, Tom Holland told Fandango that he'd love to face off against Morbius, The Living Vampire at some point down the line. Holland also went onto say that he hopes to act alongside Tobey Maguire and Andrew Garfield some day.
    • On December 9, 2021, Holland mentioned how Jon Watts had a fully fleshed-out idea for a Kraven the Hunter story, should the sequel to Spider-Man: Far From Home need it. He goes onto say that, "For a long time, there was going to be a Kraven movie that was going to be the third movie because things weren’t working out and all sorts of different stuff. Jon pitched me this Kraven movie, which actually was really cool. I don’t want to talk about it in case that movie ends up happening down the line, but it was fun." Also on this day, it was reported that with diplomatic tensions between Beijing and Washington at a nadir, Hollywood’s China business lines are hitting fresh lows. U.S. film imports are now at their lowest levels in a generation and various major studio features, including popular superhero titles Spider-Man: No Way Home and Venom: Let There Be Carnage from Sony-Marvel, still have no release dates. Their chances of getting the green light are becoming slimmer by the day as the year winds down, analysts say. However, many experts believe that the South Korean market could help fill that void left by China.
    • On December 10, 2021, Holland told Fandom that he thinks that there are some interesting villains and overall characters he'd loved Spider-Man to come across; such as Kingpin and Madame Web. In addition, he went onto say that “I also would like to maybe see what happens if my Spider-Man becomes a villain,” adds Holland. “I think there are some interesting things where you could make a film about an anti-hero, or things like that”... Holland also mentioned that the multiverse idea was "kind of a" joke that people including Kevin Feige found appealing and doable; Moreover, Holland confirmed that he, along with Andrew Garfield and Tobey Maguire, were in a group chat he started after getting both actors' numbers; Marvel and Sony also went onto deliver a new TV spot for Spider-Man: No Way Home that featured Willem Dafoe's Green Goblin and Jamie Foxx's Electro. The trailer uses scenes featuring the Green Goblin that have come in other trailers, although this one included Dafoe's villain uttering a new line that said "We have a new world to conquer."
    • The producers tried to make Michelle a superhero, after which Zendaya remarked that she was very satisfied with Watts' character work with Peter's friendship circle helping him, and "[left] the superhero-ing" to [Holland]."
    • Digital editing was done with Alfred Molina to make him look younger so he would be portrayed at the same age he was in Spider-Man 2.
    • Cut footage from Spider-Man 3 and The Amazing Spider-Man were used when Sandman and Lizard were cured.
    • This film marks Tobey Maguire's first on screen film role in seven years since Pawn Sacrifice and his first film overall in four years since The Boss Baby, the latter being a voice only role.
    • This is the first MCU film where Spider-Man is given a sound cue for his own Spider-Sense.
    • This is the third highest grossing MCU film at the global box office, only behind Avengers: Infinity War and Avengers: Endgame.
    • This is the first live-action Spider-Man film to be filmed and released fully in IMAX.

    MCU connections

    • The film has connections to Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness. Originally, Multiverse of Madness was to take place before Spider-Man: No Way Home, but after that film was delayed due to the COVID-19 pandemic, aspects of the plot of No Way Home had to be rewritten for continuity purposes. A trailer for Multiverse of Madness is included after the film's credits.
    • The film features a cameo appearance by Matt Murdock, the protagonist of the Netflix series Daredevil, as Peter Parker's lawyer. It is the third time that a character from a Marvel Television project would appear in a Marvel Studios project, being preceded by Edwin Jarvis in Avengers: Endgame and Wilson Fisk/Kingpin in Hawkeye, the latter of whom officially reprised the role the same week this film released, although he made an uncredited cameo appearance in the episode Echoes.
    • Likewise, the film features multiple glimpses of advertisements for Rogers: The Musical, an in-universe Broadway show seen in Hawkeye. No Way Home also ends with Parker swinging in his new suit above the large skating rink at the Rockefeller Center, a key location in that series' finale episode.
    • Doctor Strange uses runes to enact magic spells, specifically the Runes of Kof-Kol, just as Wanda Maximoff and Agatha Harkness did in the WandaVision episodes Previously On and The Series Finale.

    Comics references

    • The movie's storyline adapts some elements of the infamous Spider-Man: One More Day crossover, where Spider-Man, after unmasking himself during the Civil War crossover, seeks Doctor Strange's help to restore Aunt May's life after she is shot by an assassin hired by Kingpin only for Strange to be unable to do so and lead Parker to make a deal with Mephisto. In the film, Peter comes to Strange to request him to erase the memories of all people that he is Spider-Man which borrows elements from the One Moment In Time event, the sequel to One More Day which revealed how the events of Peter's deal with Mephisto went about which Strange was involved with (which both these storyline events were written by Joe Quesada). Unlike the comics where she survived, in the film, Aunt May is killed by the Green Goblin; In the climax of the film Peter is forced to change the spell so everyone forgets who he is completely, while in the comics, only the knowledge of his secret identity was erased from almost everyone; Therefore, Spider-Man wearing a black version of his costume in this film actually is a reference to Spider-Man 3 in a roundabout way; Spider-Man switches to a black version of his costume during his first encounter with Electro and Sandman. While this references the outfit that Peter wore when he was first possessed by the Venom symbiote, he also wore his own version in the comics in the fallout of Civil War as a result of his secret identity being exposed, until after his secret identity was restored. That story is called Back in Black, which was published in 2007 to coincide with the newly-released Spider-Man 3, which also featured Peter Parker in a black costume.
    • During J. Jonah Jameson's broadcast revealing Peter Parker's identity as Spider-Man and branding him a murderer, Jameson repeatedly refers to the hero as the "Spider-Menace." This nickname is a direct reference to the iconic derogatory term frequently used for Spider-Man in the comics.
    • During Peter's interrogation by Agent Cleary, MJ rejects the last name "Watson" when asked and expresses concern about her father's reaction to Peter being Spider-Man. These moments reference the complicated and often abusive relationship between Mary Jane Watson and her father in the comics, where she frequently expressed disdain for him and preferred to distance herself from the Watson name.
    • Matt Murdock serves as Peter Parker's lawyer and helps handle his defense following the revelation of his identity as Spider-Man. This mirrors a storyline from Amazing Spider-Man #219, in which Murdock also represented Peter Parker in court. Murdock has frequently appeared as a lawyer for Spider-Man or Peter Parker across various comic book runs.
    • When a Mysterio supporter throws green paint on Spider-Man's suit and accuses him of being a murderer, a blue store sign reading "Ramos" is visible in the background behind Peter. This is a reference to artist Humberto Ramos, who is well-known for his acclaimed work on various Spider-Man comic series.
    • On the rooftop of Midtown School of Science and Technology, while Peter Parker and Michelle Jones are talking, graffiti tags reading "Ditko" and "Gkane" can be seen on the wall behind them. "Ditko" references Steve Ditko, the co-creator of Spider-Man and Doctor Strange, while "Gkane" is a nod to artist Gil Kane, who illustrated many classic Spider-Man stories. Steve Ditko's name also appears on the side of the F.E.A.S.T. truck that Aunt May drives.
    • The shelter where Aunt May works is the F.E.A.S.T. Community Center. This is a direct reference to her comic book counterpart, who began working at the F.E.A.S.T. Center during the Brand New Day storyline, the immediate follow-up to One More Day.
    • The plot of Spider-Man meeting and teaming up with his Friendly Neighborhood and Amazing Spider-Man versions also has some similarities to previous Spider-Man crossovers, the 2012 comic book storyline Spider-Men (which involved the mainstream version of Spider-Man crossing over to the Ultimate Marvel universe where he meets Miles Morales, and the loved ones of his "deceased" younger Ultimate Marvel counterpart) and 2014 storyline Spider-Verse (where different versions of Spider-Man team-up to deal with a common threat).
    • When Ned Leeds learns from Friendly Neighborhood Spider-Man how his best friend (Harry Osborn) became a villain and died while trying to kill him, Leeds promises he won't do the same if he were put in that position, which is a reference to his comic book counterpart who was the Hobgoblin.
    • Similar to Spider-Man: Homecoming and Spider-Man: Far From Home, several easter eggs appear on vehicle license plates during the traffic jam scene as Peter searches for the MIT Assistant Vice Chancellor: The MIT Assistant Vice Chancellor's license plate reads "63A5M-3", referencing Amazing Spider-Man #3 released in 1963, the first appearance of Doctor Octopus. Coincidentally, Doctor Octopus later throws the car; A nearby vehicle has the license plate "TCR 339", likely referencing Timely Comics, Marvel's predecessor, founded on January 3, 1939; A yellow taxi displays the number "1228", referencing December 28, Stan Lee's birthday and the birthdate of Spider-Man's co-creator; As Doctor Octopus destroys the highway bridge, a yellow car with the license plate "ASMA2" can be seen, referencing Amazing Spider-Man Annual #2, which features Doctor Strange teaming up with Spider-Man; When Doctor Octopus grabs Spider-Man and throws him off the bridge, a car with the license plate "6306 ASM" appears, referencing The Amazing Spider-Man #6 released in 1963, which depicts the origin of the Lizard. The two characters later encounter the Lizard at the Sanctum Sanctorum.
    • During the struggle for the Macchina di Kadavus, Doctor Strange forces Peter Parker into an astral projection, separating his consciousness from his body. Even while Peter's astral form watches, his physical body—guided purely by his spider-sense—continues to evade Strange and keep the box away from him. Throughout the scene, almost invisible wavy lines can be seen emanating above Peter's astral head, visually representing the iconic Spider-Sense effect from the comics.
    • After being cured of his powers by Doctor Octopus and reconciling with the Spider-Man of his home universe, Max Dillon speaks with Peter-3. Having assumed that the masked hero he idolized was black—based on his neighborhood in Queens and the communities he protected, Dillon expresses surprise upon seeing his face and remarks that there must be a black Spider-Man somewhere out there. This line serves as a subtle reference to Miles Morales.
    • As Doctor Strange attempts to seal the unstable multiversal rifts caused by the botched spell, various silhouettes of Spider-Man-related characters can be seen through the cracks in reality. Among them are Black Cat, Scorpion, Kraven the Hunter, Rhino, and the Six-Armed Spider-Man. This moment teases numerous potential variants from across the Multiverse.
    • Spider-Man's temporary alliance with the displaced villains is a reference to the first iteration of the Ultimate Six of Earth-1610. Additionally counting Spider-Man, the lineup is nearly identical with the Lizard taking the place of Kraven the Hunter.

    Pop culture

    • The film features the incarnations of Spider-Man, Green Goblin, Doctor Octopus, and Sandman that appeared in Sam Raimi's Spider-Man trilogy. Mary Jane Watson, Ben Parker, Dennis Carradine, Harry Osborn/New Goblin, Curt Connors, Penny Marko, and Eddie Brock/Venom from that universe are mentioned.
    • The film features the incarnations of Spider-Man, Lizard, and Electro that appeared in Marc Webb's The Amazing Spider-Man films. Gwen Stacy, Ben Parker, and Aleksei Sytsevich/Rhino from that universe are mentioned.
    • Amazing Spider-Man saves Michelle from a long fall when Spider-Man was intercepted by the Green Goblin. This calls back to a notable scene from The Amazing Spider-Man 2, where Amazing Spider-Man was unable to save Gwen Stacy as she was falling to her demise.
    • Friendly Neighborhood Spider-Man experiences a bad backache before the final battle. This calls back to Spider-Man 2, where he attempts to jump from building-to-building to reawaken his powers, exclaiming "I'm back! I'm back!" but instead falls, hitting a parked car below and gets up groaning "My back! My back!". This is also a reference to Tobey Maguire's real-world back pain which led to rampant tabloid speculation and rumors that the role would be recast in the sequel with Jake Gyllenhaal.
    • When Peter-3 claims he is "lame" compared to the other Peters, Peter-2 reassures him that he is "amazing," referencing the title of his film series, The Amazing Spider-Man. On the official Marvel website, Peter-3 was officially nicknamed "The Amazing Spider-Man."
    • "You know, I'm something of a scientist myself." is a quote that was spoken by Norman Osborn in Spider-Man, which appears in Spider-Man: No Way Home.
    • Doctor Octopus' catchphrase "The power of the sun, in the palm of my hand...", which first appeared in Spider-Man 2, is said twice in Spider-Man: No Way Home.
    • Tobey's Spider-Man calling Andrew's Spider-Man 'amazing' is a reference to "The Amazing Spider-Man" movies, in which Andrew starred in.
    • When the Green Goblin arrives at the Statue of Liberty, he says "Can the Spider-Man come out to play?". He says the same line in Spider-Man, when Peter Parker attempts to leave a message for Mary Jane Watson and learns of the Goblin kidnapping her.
    • When the Friendly Neighborhood Spider-Man reunites with Otto Octavius the latter questions how the visibly older Peter Parker is doing and he responds "Trying to do better." the same line is spoken when the pair first meet in Spider-Man 2.
    • The Spider-Sense sound cue from Sam Raimi's trilogy is heard when the Friendly Neighborhood Spider-Man detects the Green Goblin flying in to attack the group in the final battle.
    • Before Spider-Man can kill Green Goblin (by stabbing him with the blades of his glider), Friendly Neighborhood Spider-Man jumps in to stop him from doing it. The scene of the two struggling resembles the finale of Spider-Man where Friendly Neighborhood Spider-Man stuggles to hold back a spear that Green Goblin is trying to stab him with. After Peter finally gives, Green Goblin takes advantage of the moment to stab Friendly Neighborhood Spider-Man in the back, in a scene closely resembling Harry Osborn's sacrifice at the end of Spider-Man 3.
    • The sketch with the Amazing Spider-Man in the credits features a Spider-Man Suit that resembles the one from The Amazing Spider-Man.
    • When Tobey Maguire's name appears in the end credits, it is accompanied by a sketch of him with half of his face covered by his Spider-Man mask. This is almost identical to the iconic promotional poster for Spider-Man 2.
