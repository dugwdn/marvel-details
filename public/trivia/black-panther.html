    Black Panther trivia

    Background details, easter eggs and behind-the-scenes facts fans have spotted in Black Panther.

    Spotted on screenScene detailsMCU connectionsComics references

    Spotted on screen

    • Final battle The last fight takes place on an underground railway, a literal underground railroad. Spotted by Twitter user @RussellHFilm, reported by buzzfeed.com
    • Shuri's lab Shuri's self-lacing sneakers reference Back to the Future Part II, an old American movie she says T'Chaka loved. Reported by buzzfeed.com
    • Shuri mocks T'Challa's sandals The sandals she laughs at are Alexander McQueen shoes, per costume designer Ruth E. Carter. Reported by buzzfeed.com
    • 1992 flashback Young T'Chaka is played by Atandwa Kani, the real son of John Kani, who plays the older T'Chaka. Reported by buzzfeed.com
    • Busan casino Stan Lee is credited as Thirsty Gambler. Reported by buzzfeed.com
    • Busan casino outfits T'Challa, Okoye and Nakia wear black, red and green, the colors of the Pan-African flag. Reported by buzzfeed.com
    • T'Challa walks through Wakanda Rapper Wale makes a brief cameo. Reported by buzzfeed.com
    • Klaue's interrogation A bearded man who looks like Captain America is on a screen in the background. Reported by buzzfeed.com
    • T'Challa's new suit The glowing purple patterns on the suit carry Wakandan writing that a Reddit user translated as I love you mom. Spotted by Reddit user u/thecruiser_, reported by screenrant.com
    • Warrior Falls challenge, when T'Challa is crowned The stripe painted across T'Challa's forehead is a nod to Rafiki marking Simba in The Lion King, per Ryan Coogler. Reported by screenrant.com

    Scene details

    • Ryan Coogler brought in consultants who are experts on African history and politics to work on defining Wakanda: "There are many African countries, each with different histories, mythologies, and cultures; there are several tribes who live amongst each other, and together they make the identity of their country. We honed in on some of the history/cultural influences from African countries and then made estimations."
    • The filmmakers cite The Godfather films and the James Bond films as an influence on the story, calling it "a big, operatic family drama centered on a world of international espionage".
    • Daniel Kaluuya described Black Panther as "Marvel's Game of Thrones."
    • Production designer Hannah Beachler mentioned Blade Runner as one the main source of inspiration for Wakanda's futuristic design.
    • Ryan Coogler describe the film's central theme as responsibility and identity: "What do the powerful owe those in need? It separates the good-guys from the villains. What value is strength unless you're using it to help someone? Wakanda pretends to be just another struggling African country, but some of its neighbours are struggling for real. If Wakandans don't stand up for themselves, who will? But if they stand only for themselves, then who are they?"
    • Ryan Coogler and Joe Robert Cole considered the possibility of introducing Eli Bradley in the film, but they ultimately dropped the idea in favor of focusing on Wakandan characters more.
    • Ryan Coogler wished to add Spider-Man villain Kraven the Hunter to the movie, though due to legal issues regarding the deal with Sony, he couldn't feature him.
    • Black Panther was the first of the Marvel characters whose movie rights reverted to Marvel, having been previously owned by Artisan Entertainment, Columbia Pictures, and 20th Century Fox. Marvel Studios got the rights back in 2005. The character almost had a film in the 90's starring Wesley Snipes; The character would have been in the third or fourth Fantastic Four film directed by Tim Story, and was considered to be played by Djimon Hounsou.
    • Black Panther is stated to be "very honest and gritty" film by Michael B. Jordan (the actor who portrays Erik Killmonger) unlike other MCU films.
    • Black Panther is the first MCU film since Captain America: Civil War to include a prologue scene ahead of any studio logos, though Spider-Man: Homecoming included one between the Sony logos and the Marvel Studios logo.
    • The post-credits caption promises "Black Panther will return in Avengers: Infinity War".
    • Black Panther is one of the only four MCU films not featuring Iron Man (along with Captain Marvel, Spider-Man: Far From Home, Spider-Man: No Way Home and Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness) that grossed $1 billion and $900 million worldwide.
    • Black Panther was credited with helping to save the life of Xavier Cunningham, an eleven-year-old boy who was impaled on a barbecue skewer following a fall from his treehouse; Xavier stated he knew not to remove the skewer as he remembered that Erik Killmonger died only after removing the spear he'd been stabbed with.
    • On November 29, 2020, the Marvel Studios logo animation on the Disney+ version of the film was replaced with a tribute to Chadwick Boseman, similar to the one for Stan Lee in Captain Marvel. Boseman would have turned 44 on that date.

    MCU connections

    • Second post-credits scene Children in Wakanda call Bucky White Wolf, the name of T'Challa's adopted brother in the comics. Reported by buzzfeed.com
    • Killmonger's plan His contacts are in London, New York and Hong Kong, the three cities with Doctor Strange's sanctums. Reported by buzzfeed.com

    Comics references

    • Characters from the comics adapted from the movie in the main cast are Erik Killmonger, Nakia, Okoye, Shuri, Ramonda, Zuri, M'Baku, and W'Kabi.
    • The movie also features locations such as Birnin Zana, Jabari Village, Warrior Falls, and Djalia, from the Black Panther comic books.
    • One of the lines that T'Challa utters to Klaue "every breath you take is mercy from me" was used in New Avengers #22.
    • T'Challa facing a rhinoceros was taken from Jungle Action #9.
    • Killmonger becoming a version of the Black Panther, has precedence in Marvel comics history.
