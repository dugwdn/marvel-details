    Scene details

    • The film shares several notable narrative parallels with Pixar's The Incredibles (both being Disney-produced superhero films). These include: The protagonists, due to arrogance, previously ignored enthusiastic fans seeking their attention or partnership. Tony Stark dismissed Aldrich Killian and his ideas at a New Year's Eve party, while Mr. Incredible rejected young Buddy Pine (who wanted to become his sidekick "IncrediBoy") and focused on his solo heroic life; Years later, new threats emerge involving advanced technology, with the scorned fans operating as the masterminds from the shadows (Trevor Slattery as the public face of the Mandarin for Killian; the Omnidroid as Syndrome's creation); As adults, both Killian and Syndrome harbor deep resentment toward their former idols for the past rejection; The villains orchestrate the capture of the heroes by invading or compromising their respective homes/bases. Tony is overpowered and captured by Eric Savin after a confrontation, while Mr. Incredible is ensnared in Syndrome's trap involving sticky inflatable rubber balls; The villains later reveal their intense hatred for the heroes and their grand plans. Killian's scheme involves seizing hidden geopolitical control over the United States by perpetuating an endless cycle of war and terrorism to profit from selling Extremis technology to all sides. Syndrome's plan centers on "democratizing" superpowers through technology, rendering traditional superheroes obsolete; Both villains have key female assistants who eventually turn against them upon seeing their true nature (Maya Hansen with Killian and Mirage with Syndrome). Maya, however, is killed shortly after her betrayal; During the Battle on the Norco, the heroes' best friends with dark skin become involved without their primary suits or powers initially (James Rhodes for Tony and Frozone for Mr. Incredible). Unlike Frozone, Rhodes dons his War Machine armor to help protect the President and others amid the chaos; In the finale, the protagonists receive crucial help from allies to defeat the main villain and associated forces, learning they do not have to handle everything alone. Tony is aided by the Iron Legion and Pepper Potts (who gains Extremis abilities), while Mr. Incredible is supported by his family (the Parrs). In both cases, this teamwork leads to victory.
    • When signing autographs for two children, Tony Stark comments that one of the boys resembles Ralphie Parker from the 1983 holiday classic A Christmas Story. The child actor who portrayed Ralphie, Peter Billingsley, served as an executive producer on Iron Man and Iron Man 2. He also appeared in the MCU as William Ginter Riva in Iron Man (and reprised the role in Spider-Man: Far From Home).
    • During the final battle, Iron Man is pinned down by rubble and defends himself by extending a spike from his arm. The blocking and camera angle closely mirror a similar scene in RoboCop.
    • Although Iron Man 3 is the third solo Iron Man film, this marks the fifth time Robert Downey Jr. portrayed Iron Man. He previously made a cameo appearance as Stark in The Incredible Hulk and appeared as part of the ensemble cast in The Avengers. At the time of filming, Downey's contract with Marvel covered only two Avengers sequels, but he expressed a strong desire to continue playing the character, stating he would "hate to see someone fill his shoes" and that he would keep returning "as long as the films are great." His contract was later extended to include Captain America: Civil War.
    • Shane Black described the film as "a Tom Clancy thriller", with the focus on real-world type villains and not "two men in iron suits fighting each other".
    • Kevin Feige described the film as "a full-on Tony Stark-centric movie... He's stripped of everything, he's backed up against a wall, and he's gotta use his intelligence to get out of it. He can't call Thor, Cap or Nick Fury, and he can't look for the Helicarrier in the sky." Robert Downey Jr. further described the character as influenced by a post-The Avengers world: "What are his challenges now? What are some limitations that might be placed on him and what sort of threat would have him, as usual, ignore those limitations?".
    • Robert Downey Jr. had previously starred in Shane Black's film, Kiss Kiss Bang Bang, and spoke highly of Black replacing Jon Favreau, saying that "bringing in Shane Black to write and direct the film is basically the only thing that Favreau and the audience and Marvel and I could ever actually sign off on."
    • According to producer Kevin Feige, the Mandarin is portrayed with an ambiguous background: "It's less about his specific ethnicity than the analogy of various cultures and their iconography that he perverts for his own end." For instance, the Mandarin sports a "Captain America Shield" tattoo, in effect using the symbol to represent his own leadership of an army.
    • Rebecca Hall was unhappy that she was led to believe Maya Hansen would have a bigger role, only for the character to be killed off before the third act.
    • Shane Black doesn't appear to be too fond of the scenes that were shot exclusively for the Chinese cut of the film, claiming that he wasn't involved in any of them.
    • This movie marks the first time Samuel L. Jackson and Clark Gregg do not appear in an Iron Man related movie as Nick Fury and Phil Coulson, respectively.
    • This film is the third media adaptation to use the Iron Man: Extremis storyline. The first was the six-part Iron Man: Extremis motion comic released in 2010, which directly adapted the original six-issue comic book miniseries by Warren Ellis and Adi Granov. The second was the toned-down version used in the Season 2 episode "Extremis" of the child-friendly animated series Iron Man: Armored Adventures, which aired on March 14, 2012.
    • This film is frequently cited as the highest-grossing film set at Christmas time, with a worldwide box office total of over $1.215 billion. While traditional Christmas movies (such as Home Alone or The Grinch) are often ranked separately, the film's December setting has led many sources to include it among — or at the top of — lists of the most successful Christmas-set films in history.
    • A post-credits caption promises "Tony Stark will return". This is the first caption not to refer to a specific movie.
    • This was the final film appearance of Joan Rivers before her death in September 2014.
    • This was the final film in which Tony Stark appeared without Steve Rogers also appearing. Every subsequent film featuring Stark also includes Rogers.
    • While he appeared onscreen in TV shows and voice performance following the release of the film some posthumously this ultimately proved to be Miguel Ferrer's final onscreen film role before his death in January 2017.

    MCU connections

    • Tony Stark is shown suffering from post-traumatic stress disorder following his near-death experience at the end of The Avengers, where he flew a nuclear missile through a wormhole.
    • The ring worn on the Mandarin's right pinky finger is the same ring worn by Raza on his right hand in Iron Man.
    • Tony Stark and Pepper Potts are now living together, having become romantically involved at the end of Iron Man 2.
    • The Mandarin has a tattoo of Captain America's shield on his neck. However, the shield is not emblazoned with a star, but an anarchist's "A". Mandarin is said that he dons symbols like those of heroes as a practice of irony.
    • When giving advice to Harley Keener with daddy issues, Tony Stark awkwardly tells him "no need to be a pussy about it," nodding to his own unresolved issues with his father, Howard Stark, which were explored in Iron Man 2.
    • When discussing how to sway public opinion, Aldrich Killian tells Tony Stark, "Nothing's been the same ever since that big guy with the hammer fell out of the sky," referencing Thor's arrival on Earth and the events of Thor, in which Mjolnir also fell from the sky. A deleted scene titled "Hey, Is That Thor?" features Eric Savin (wearing the Iron Patriot armor) aboard Air Force One, where he asks a passenger, "Hey, is that Thor?" before knocking him out.
    • In the mid-credits scene, Bruce Banner is seen at Tony Stark's house, referencing the two driving away together at the end of The Avengers.

    Comics references

    • According to the writers, the storyline is influenced by the Iron Man comic miniseries 'Extremis' by Warren Ellis, which involved Tony Stark developing and using the Extremis virus, a nanotech version of Captain America's serum. The origin story in the first Iron Man film was also influenced by parts of 'Extremis'.
    • War Machine's new armor in the film is named the Iron Patriot armor. In the mainstream Marvel Comics, the Iron Patriot identity was originally used by Norman Osborn. In the Ultimate Marvel universe, it was used by Tony Stark. Around the time of the film's release, the mainstream version of James Rhodes began wearing the Iron Patriot armor in the comics.
    • The Iron Patriot armor in this film is a new set of armor that uses Captain America's red, white, and blue color scheme. In the comics, the Iron Patriot identity was originally used by Norman Osborn (the Green Goblin), Spider-Man's nemesis. In the film, the Iron Patriot armor is simply an upgraded and repainted version of the War Machine armor.

    Behind the scenes

    • Gemma Arterton, Diane Kruger and Isla Fisher were considered for a role. Jessica Chastain was cast but she dropped out due to scheduling conflicts.
    • Jude Law was considered for the role of Aldrich Killian. He later played Yon-Rogg in Captain Marvel.
    • Andy Lau was in talks to play the role of Chen Lu (Radioactive Man), but dropped out upon the birth of his first child. Daoming Chen and Wu Xiubowere considered for the role. Finally Wang Xueqi was cast and the role was changed to the unrelated Doctor Wu.
    • This is the first film in Phase 2 of the Marvel Cinematic Universe.
    • This film, along with The Amazing Spider-Man 2, is the last film to use the classic white rectangle Marvel logo.
    • Originally slated for distribution by Paramount Pictures, the film was transferred to Walt Disney Studios Motion Pictures following Disney's acquisition of Marvel Entertainment. As with The Avengers, Paramount remains credited as a distributor for contractual reasons.
    • Originally, Marvel Studios and Walt Disney Pictures considered filming Iron Man 3 in Michigan, attracted by the state's film tax incentives. However, after Michigan declined to meet the production's requested incentive amount (reportedly up to $30 million), North Carolina guaranteed a $20 million tax credit. The producers ultimately chose to shoot the film primarily in Wilmington, North Carolina, at EUE/Screen Gems Studios and various local locations.
    • This is the first Iron Man film in the Marvel Cinematic Universe not directed by Jon Favreau. Favreau declined to return as director due to creative conflicts with Marvel Studios during the production of Iron Man 2, though he agreed to reprise his role as Happy Hogan.
    • One scene was shot inside Epic Games, a video game development company known for the Gears of War franchise.
    • Production was delayed on August 15, 2012, when Robert Downey Jr.'s ankle and neck were injured in a stunt.
    • In an interview regarding Spider-Man: No Way Home, Kevin Feige revealed that Happy Hogan was originally planned to die in this film. Jon Favreau insisted that Hogan end up in a coma instead of dying.
    • The destruction of Stark's Malibu mansion was filmed at the EUE/Screen Gems Studios water tank.
    • This is the first film in the Marvel Cinematic Universe to include a teaser-type scene ahead of the studio logos.
    • This film includes a logo animation for Marvel in general rather than Marvel Studios specifically.
