    Spider-Man: Brand New Day trivia

    Background details, easter eggs and behind-the-scenes facts fans have spotted in Spider-Man: Brand New Day.

    Scene detailsComics referencesBehind the scenesPop culture

    Scene details

    • The film's theatrical release coincides with the 10th anniversary of Captain America: Civil War, that introduced Tom Holland as Spider-Man and marked his debut in the shared universe. Likewise, the film released a few months after the 10th anniversary of the second season of Daredevil, in which The Punisher made his MCU debut.
    • A prominent piano melody plays in the background during key emotional and action beats, echoing the iconic Spider-Man theme originating from the 1967 animated series.
    • Reportedly, there was a lot of conflict between Marvel and Sony over if the film should be a multiverse movie like Spider-Man: No Way Home, specifically a crossover featuring Venom from Sony's Spider-Man Universe, or a street-based movie like Spider-Man: Homecoming and Spider-Man: Far From Home, with Marvel and Tom Holland wanting the latter, and Sony wanting the former. Marvel and Holland ultimately won, with Brand New Day being confirmed to be a street-based movie.
    • Despite Jon Bernthal having a clause where he'd only portray The Punisher in dark and gritty stories to honor the character as much as possible, he made a special exception for this film since he was a legitimate fan of Tom Holland and his portrayal of Spider-Man. The two of them even helped each other out for their respective audition tapes for Marvel, with Bernthal being the one to suggest Holland do a backflip to impress the executives, thus netting him the role of Spider-Man.
    • Having waited nearly a decade to return to the character since his debut in Homecoming, Michael Mando took to Instagram to reveal his enthusiasm for playing Mac Gargan once again, showing a picture of him with a Scorpion tattoo on his neck to celebrate.
    • Tom Holland pitched an unspecified idea for the film at one point, but it was rejected by Marvel because the general concept was already being utilized in Captain America: Brave New World.
    • Kevin Feige reportedly lobbied hard to include Miles Morales in film, but Sony Pictures rejected the request. The studio did not want Marvel Studios to use the character in live-action until the animated Spider-Verse trilogy was completed.
    • Destin Daniel Cretton was originally set to direct the fifth Avengers movie, then titled The Kang Dynasty, but he was replaced by The Russo Brothers and the film was retitled Avengers: Doomsday. To compensate, Marvel brought Cretton on to direct Brand New Day instead.
    • The film initial trailer was preceded by members of the Spider-Man community from different parts of the world sharing a few seconds of the trailer alongside their connection with Spider-Man on Instagram, starting and ending with Tom Holland from London. The members were as follows: Cliver Huaman Sanchez from Lima, Peru; OSUSpidey from Colombus, Ohio; Alex Arellano from Mexico City; Sarah Ridgle from Las Vegas, Nevada; Keone Young from Honolulu, Hawaii; Ben Boyce from Auckland, New Zealand; Bianca Bella and Christian Pillirone from Melbourne, Australia; Shido Nakamura from Tokyo, Japan; Mark from Seoul, South Korea; Lean Mean Spidey Machine from Singapore; Spider-Verse Indonesia from Indonesia; Dastan Mukhamedrakhim from Almaty, Kazakhstan; Ashish Chanchlani from Mumbai, India; Burak Göktaş from Istanbul, Türkiye; Henry Weckström from Turku, Finland; Ndlovu Youth Choir from Limpopo, South Africa; Dannero from Munich, Germany; Mattia Villardita from Rome, Italy; Esteban Ocon from Paris, France; Santiago Jiménez Treviño from Oviedo, Spain; Dylan Hughes from Wales, United Kingdom; Wirley Contaifer from Rio de Janeiro, Brazil; Gaucho Araña from Buenos Aires, Argentina.
    • The film's initial trailer released on March 18, 2026 became the biggest trailer launch in history with 718.6 million views in 24 hours, breaking Deadpool & Wolverine's record. It crossed 1 billion views by March 24, 2026, marking history as the first trailer to do so.
    • The film's theatrical release coincides with the 18th anniversary of Sony SAB's Hindi sitcom Taarak Mehta Ka Ooltah Chashmah. Spider-Man made an appearance in the 4777th and 4778th episodes of the sitcom to commemorate the event.
    • Tom Holland's own non-alcoholic beer brand BERO appears in Delmar's IV Deli-Grocery.

    Comics references

    • The title Spider-Man: Brand New Day is a reference to the 2008 comic book storyline of the same name, in which Peter Parker's status quo is reset following his deal with Mephisto in the "One More Day" arc, restoring his secret identity and saving his aunt May Parker at the cost of undoing many of his past relationships, including his marriage to Mary Jane Watson.
    • The Punisher's presence in the film is a reference to how he made his first appearance in Amazing Spider-Man #129, where he was initially depicted as an antagonist hired to assassinate Spider-Man by the Jackal before he became the anti-hero we know him as today.
    • Spider-Man swinging through the city while carrying a member of the Manfredi Crime Family pays a homage to Amazing Fantasy #15, which marked Spider-Man's original debut in comics.
    • Spider-Man dodges Boomerang's razor-sharp boomerang projectiles while airborne, recreating the iconic cover of Amazing Spider-Man #345, which features a similar dynamic evasion pose against Boomerang.
    • Spider-Man battling Tarantula while clinging to a wall of ship's side, with two New York City Police Department officers nearby observing or involved in the scene, directly recreating the iconic cover of Amazing Spider-Man #134, Tarantula's first appearance issue, which depicts a similar dynamic wall-clinging battle pose and composition.
    • Spider-Man battling Ramrod right by a wall, is a recreation of the iconic cover of Amazing Spider-Man #221.
    • Spider-Man fighting the Hand is a a homage to the cover of New Avengers Vol #27, with Spider-Man replacing Ronin and Snow replacing Elektra.
    • ASM-19 on Mac Gargan's mugshot is a reference to Amazing Spider-Man #19, which introduced Mac Gargan.
    • ASM-14 on the Hulk Inhibitor Device is a reference to Amazing Spider-Man #14, which introduced the Green Goblin and featured the Hulk.
    • The TU #8 on the Chitauri jar is a reference to The Ultimates #8, which introduced the Chitauri.
    • MJ refers to him as a "friendly neighbor" from across the hall after he delivers flowers as a housewarming gift, alluding to Spider-Man's iconic "friendly neighborhood" moniker from the comics.
    • The Punisher carrying a gravely wounded Spider-Man to Metro-General Hospital after accidentally shooting him pays a homage to the panel from Civil War #5.
    • The The Blind Spot logo is a reference to the bar of the same name that first appears in Amazing Spider-Man #547. Coincidentally, that issue is a part of the Brand New Day storyline that the movie adapts.

    Behind the scenes

    • Spider-Man: Brand New Day is the first film of the second Spider-Man trilogy starring Tom Holland.
    • Brand New Day's release marked Tom Holland as the first actor to have portrayed Peter Parker/Spider-Man in four standalone films.
    • Tom Holland and his co-star Jon Bernthal helped each other audition for their respective MCU roles as Peter Parker/Spider-Man and Frank Castle/Punisher while the pair were filming Pilgrimage in 2015. They also worked together again in The Odyssey (along with Zendaya), which released two weeks before this movie.
    • Sadie Sink previously worked with Destin Daniel Cretton in The Glass Castle.
    • During a July 2025 roundtable interview promoting The Fantastic Four: First Steps, Kevin Feige revealed that director Destin Daniel Cretton has "eight or nine comic covers" pinned up in the film's art department, which he is actively "bringing to life" in Brand New Day. Feige described this approach as "super cool" while discussing the movie's street-level focus and the inclusion of The Punisher (whose comic debut ties back to Spider-Man).
    • On August 12, 2026, screenwriters Chris McKenna and Erik Sommers revealed that Dmitri Smerdyakov/Chameleon was originally intended to be the main villain, but the idea was scrapped due to the character's previous appearance in Kraven the Hunter.

    Pop culture

    • The official poster, which features reflections of MJ and the New York City skyline within Spider-Man’s eyes, serves as a direct homage to the classic poster for Spider-Man 2. However, it presents several notable differences: In the right eye’s reflection, MJ appears with an expression reminiscent of Mary Jane Watson from the Spider-Man 2 poster, replacing Doctor Octopus’ presence from the original design; The composition focuses solely on Spider-Man’s face, rather than showing his full mask and upper torso as seen in the 2004 poster; The cityscape is depicted during a vibrant sunset, contrasting with the bright daylight setting featured in the Spider-Man 2 poster.
    • Spider-Man sits upside down on the side of a building while watching Ned record a video about MIT is a direct reference to Spider-Man: Across the Spider-Verse, where Miles Morales and Gwen Stacy are shown sitting upside down on a building, looking out over the city.
    • Spider-Man is shown diving/falling from a skyscraper in a dynamic free-fall with his suit rippling dramatically in the wind, echoing the stylish airborne descents of Spider-Man in The Amazing Spider-Man 2, where he frequently glides and dives through the New York City skyline in high-flying action beats.
    • Frank running down Spider-Man with his van is filmed in a locked-off (static camera) style, similar to the moment in The Amazing Spider-Man 2, where Spider-Man is struck by a police truck during the Oscorp chase sequence.
    • Spider-Man webs up Frank Castle's mouth to prevent him from swearing, mirroring the scene in Spider-Man, where Spider-Man webs J. Jonah Jameson's mouth shut while rescuing him from the Green Goblin.
    • Spider-Man being publicly awarded the key to the city in a ceremonial event echoes a similar moment in Spider-Man 3, where Spider-Man is honored with the key to the city by Gwen Stacy after he rescues her from a falling structure during a public celebration.
