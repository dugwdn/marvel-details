Wanda's Grief Manifests as Power: The Darkhold Connection

Why losing Vision corrupted her in the Multiverse of Madness.

Difficulty: Intermediate Read time: 12 minutes Movies involved: 5

Wanda's power grows stronger every time she suffers loss. The Darkhold doesn't corrupt her—it just makes her desperate power audible. Explore how grief and trauma fuel her reality-bending abilities.

Level 1: Casual Reading
Grief as Fuel

Wanda starts as an orphan who volunteered for Hydra experiments. She gains telekinesis and telepathy. Then she loses Vision in Infinity War, and he stays dead even after the Blip reverses. She creates WandaVision to bring him back. By Multiverse of Madness, she's using the Darkhold to find a universe where Vision is alive, and she's willing to kill everyone in her way. That's a huge turn from trying to be a hero in Civil War. Her grief corrupted her.

Level 2: Deeper Analysis
Trauma-Powered Abilities

Wanda's power is directly connected to emotional intensity, specifically grief and loss. Every major power increase happens after trauma: After her parents die (origin), she volunteers for Hydra. After Sokovia is destroyed, she leaves Hydra and joins the Avengers (larger purpose). After Vision dies, she becomes powerful enough to create WandaVision (reality-bending on a city scale). By Multiverse of Madness, she's more powerful than Doctor Strange.

The pattern is clear: grief fuels her abilities. The Darkhold doesn't corrupt her—it gives voice to powers she already has. The Darkhold contains knowledge about the multiverse and shows her that she could access universes where Vision lives. But the Darkhold doesn't make her willing to kill for this goal; it just makes her believe it's possible. Her desperation was always there. In WandaVision, she created an entire fake reality to keep Vision. The Darkhold simply showed her she could do this across multiverses. The 'corruption' is her choosing desperation over morality—which might have happened without the Darkhold, just more slowly.

Level 3: Expert Theory
Unlimited Power Meets Finite Wisdom

Wanda's arc is a tragic study in the ethics of power without wisdom. The Mind Stone activated latent abilities in her, but those abilities aren't separate from her consciousness—they're expressions of her consciousness. Telepathy emerged from her need to understand (connecting with others). Telekinesis emerged from her need to protect (moving objects to defend). Reality manipulation emerged from her need to save (creating WandaVision). Each power is Wanda's inner need made manifest through cosmic means.

By Multiverse of Madness, she's discovered that her powers can reshape reality across infinite universes. The Darkhold doesn't grant this power; it educates her about it. But education without wisdom creates tragedy. Wanda learns she's essentially a god—she can remake universes. The Darkhold shows her the math: there are infinite universes, so there must be one where she's happy, where Vision lives, where her family is whole. The logical conclusion is: find that universe and move there. But that logic ignores that every universe's Wanda matters, not just hers. Every universe's Vision is real.

The Darkhold's corruption isn't magical—it's the corruption of unlimited power meeting finite wisdom. Wanda has god-tier reality manipulation abilities but human-level emotional development. She's spent her life losing things. The Darkhold gives her the power to lose nothing ever again—and she's willing to destroy anything that stands between her and that goal. This isn't possession or external corruption. It's Wanda's own desire amplified by power. The dangerous Wanda was always there; she was just constrained by weakness. The Darkhold simply removed her limitations. The horrifying implication: unlimited power truly requires enlightenment to wield wisely—and enlightenment is something Wanda, for all her abilities, hasn't developed yet.

The Payoff You'll understand that Wanda's villainy in Multiverse of Madness isn't character assassination—it's the inevitable tragedy of absolute power meeting desperate grief.
